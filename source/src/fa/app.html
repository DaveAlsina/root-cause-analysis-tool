New function analysis

A function analysis describes what a system must do, apart from how. It starts by deciding where the system ends.

Other languages: every box will be asked for in each one

Variants

Each variant is one function structure: its own boundary, decomposition, externals and flows. Functions are shared, so a function's text, notes, sources and tags are the same in every variant. Compare variants by moving the boundary, reordering subfunctions, or splitting and merging them.

New variant

Flow types

Every flow has a type, shown by its colour and its line pattern, so the diagram still reads when printed in black and white. The classic three are material, energy and signal; unwanted flows (inoculum, waste) keep their type and are drawn red with a hollow head.

Levels of the system

Levels are nested rings, outermost first, as in Moreira et al. (2023): landscape, farm, field, pathogen. They show scale; where an element acts along the process is its stage tag. Elements keep their level and their place in its ring when you rename, reorder or add levels; the elements of a deleted level move to the next level inwards.

How it works

The file has three modes. System maps what is there: elements on nested levels, groups, and boundaries drawn through them. Goals says what the design must achieve inside each boundary, as a tree under one main goal, scored by stakeholders. Functions works out what must happen inside a boundary: boxes are functions (verb + noun), arrows are flows (material, energy, signal). The dashed box is the overall function and the system boundary; anything outside it is an external.

Goals

A tree per boundaryThe toolbar picks the tree on screen, or starts one for a boundary that has none (empty, or with the structure of another tree). The main goal is the root of every tree
Add goalsDouble-click the canvas for a new family. Tab adds a subgoal to the selected goal; Enter renames it. Drag a goal onto another to move it under that one
ScoreHow much achieving a goal contributes to its parent, 1 (marginal) to 5 (the parent fails without it). Keys 1–5 score as the stakeholder picked in the toolbar, 0 clears; the side panel has every stakeholder's row
RelevanceAbsolute multiplies s/5 down the path; normalised divides each score by its siblings' sum, so the leaves sum to 100 %. Stakeholders combine by geometric mean, arithmetic mean or median. The ⓘ buttons in the side panel explain each
SensitivityThe ranking of the leaves shows, for each, the ranks it takes when any one score moves by ±1, and whether the top three hold
Out of scopeA goal can stay in a tree but count for nothing inside this boundary: tick Out of scope
FunctionsIn Functions, Serves goals on a function; its relevance shows beside the ★ and on its box. The checks list ★ functions with low relevance and relevant functions without ★

The method, in the side panel when nothing is selected: set the boundary, describe the black box, decompose, check the wording, add auxiliary functions, make variants. Steps tick themselves when the diagram shows they are done; you can also tick them yourself.

Wording check. A box should start with a function verb. The list holds the terms of the functional basis (Hirtz et al. 2002), their synonyms, and everyday postharvest and systems-analysis verbs, in English and Spanish. Suggestions show which basis term a synonym belongs to. Accept your own verbs from the method panel.
